Applying for opportunities in South Africa

Use these checklists to prepare before applying. Each advertised opportunity has its own rules, so always read the original notice.

How to prepare for a government job application

  1. Read the full advertisement, including qualification, experience, location and closing-date requirements.
  2. Prepare a clear CV and accurate contact details.
  3. Check whether the department requires a specific application form, such as the current Z83, and whether supporting documents are required at application or shortlisting stage.
  4. Follow the submission channel stated in that particular vacancy notice.
  5. Keep your application reference and check official communication channels.

Start with DPSA vacancy circulars.

Applying for bursaries

  1. Check whether your programme, year of study, institution and household circumstances meet the funder's eligibility criteria.
  2. Read deadlines for first-time and returning applicants separately.
  3. Prepare the documents required by the funder, such as identification and academic results, and use only their official submission channel.
  4. Keep a copy of your application or reference number.
  5. Be cautious of anyone who promises bursary approval in return for a fee.

Official starting points: Funza Lushaka and NSFAS.

Before bidding on a government tender

  1. Read the scope of work, mandatory returnable forms, evaluation criteria and clarification deadlines.
  2. Confirm your business registration and any tax, supplier-registration or compliance documents demanded by the particular bid.
  3. Calculate all delivery, labour, materials, risk and tax costs before pricing.
  4. Submit exactly in the requested format and before the official closing time.
  5. Verify amendments, cancellations and award notices on the procuring authority's original platform.

Browse tenders through the National Treasury eTender portal.

Watch out for job and bursary scams

Be suspicious of guarantees of selection, unofficial payment requests, and messages that ask for banking passwords, PINs or one-time passwords. Independently open the official provider's website rather than trusting a forwarded link.
