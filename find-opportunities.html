How to find legitimate jobs, bursaries and tenders in South Africa for free

Looking for an opportunity can take time, especially if you are unsure which websites publish official announcements. Start with primary sources and build a repeatable routine rather than relying on forwarded messages and screenshots.

Important: This guide explains how to search. It is not a job advertisement, a bursary award or an invitation to tender. Always confirm open dates, requirements and application instructions on the issuing organisation's website.

1. Find public-service job vacancies

Use the Department of Public Service and Administration's Public Service Vacancy Circulars. Open the latest circular and filter the notices by department, province, role and minimum qualifications. Different vacancies within one circular may have different closing dates and submission instructions, so read each advertisement carefully.

Prepare a current CV. If a notice asks for the Z83 application form, use the version specified by the department. Follow the application channel printed in the advertisement, rather than sending documents to addresses shared in comments on social media.

2. Check teaching bursaries and student funding

The Funza Lushaka programme is an official source of information about teacher-education bursaries. Check whether your intended teaching qualification and study choices match the programme rules before applying. For broader qualifying student aid, consult NSFAS.

Keep a list of application opening and closing dates, document requirements and reference numbers. If you are a returning applicant, do not assume your deadline matches a first-time applicant's deadline.

3. Find internships and learnerships

Search official departmental careers pages, public entities and employer careers pages. Treat a social-media post as a lead, not as proof that a vacancy exists. Check the same opportunity on the actual employer's website before sharing your ID documents or other personal information.

4. Explore government tenders

The National Treasury eTender portal is a starting point for published procurement opportunities. Read the entire tender document, mandatory eligibility conditions and evaluation criteria. Confirm the exact closing date, submission format and whether compulsory briefing sessions apply. A tender can be cancelled or amended after publication.

5. Keep your search organised

  1. Choose two or three official sources to check routinely.
  2. Save the original advertisement URL, closing date and application reference.
  3. Write a short checklist for each application rather than assuming requirements are identical.
  4. Submit early enough to resolve technical problems or missing documents.
  5. Track outcomes and use feedback to improve future applications.

6. Avoid scams

No legitimate source can guarantee that you will win a tender or receive a job or bursary. Be cautious when asked to pay a personal account, disclose an OTP, send banking passwords, or communicate only through an unofficial messaging app. When uncertain, contact the issuing organisation using details on its official site.

Frequently asked questions

Does SA Opportunity Hub charge an application fee?

No. SA Opportunity Hub is a free information directory and does not accept applications or guarantee placement. An external provider's own advertised rules still apply.

Are all opportunities on this website still open?

Not necessarily. Source pages and vacancy circulars may contain multiple listings with different dates, and updates can lag. Use the original publisher to confirm current status.

Where should I submit my application?

Follow the destination and procedure listed by the official employer, department, funder or procuring authority. Do not submit sensitive documents to SA Opportunity Hub.
